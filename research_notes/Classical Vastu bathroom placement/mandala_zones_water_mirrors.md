## 1. The deity grid: who sits where in the 81-square grid, and which household functions the texts tie to named squares or deities

### Takeaway
The 81-square (*paramaśāyika*) grid of Bṛhat Saṃhitā T 52.42–50 / K 53.42–50 (repeated almost verbatim in Matsya Purāṇa 253.19–33) seats 45 deities: Brahmā on the central 9 squares, eight around him, four more at the inner corners, and 32 on the border; the inner north-east belongs to Āpa and Āpavatsa, but the outer north-east corner is held by Śikhin (Fire) in Varāhamihira and by Īśa only in the Mayamata/Mānasāra/Agni Purāṇa family. Room functions are mostly given by the eight directions (shrine NE, kitchen SE, stores SW/NW); only the Mayamata, Mānasāra and Samarāṅgaṇa tie functions to named squares, and they are the only places where a bath (Aditi/Uditi, or east) or a latrine (Nirṛti, or "between south and south-west") is located at all, with clear conflicts between texts.

#### 1c. Conflicts between texts over the grid

- RD. Mayamata 7.33–41 (Dagens's French): border from the north-east "Īśa, Parjanya, Jayanta, Mahendraka, Āditya, Satyaka, Bhṛśa, Antarikṣa, Agni, Pūṣan, Vitatha, Rākṣasa, Yama, Gandharva, Bhṛṅgarāja, Mṛṣa, les dieux Pitṛ, Dauvārika, Sugrīva, Puṣpadanta, Jalādhipa, Asura, Śoṣa, Roga, Vāyu, Nāga, Mukhya, Bhallāṭaka, Soma, Mṛga, Aditi et Uditi"; "Āpa et Āpavatsa sont tous deux à l'intérieur, à l'angle Nord-Est; Savindra et Sāvindra… au Sud-Est; Indra et Indrarāja au Sud-Ouest, Rudra et Rudrajaya au Nord-Ouest"; "Brahmā se tient au centre… quatre dieux qui lui font face, nommés Ārya, Vivasvant, Mitra et Bhūdhara". Here the NE corner is Īśa, the SE corner Agni and the NW corner Vāyu, unlike BS/Matsya (NE Śikhin, SE Anila) — [Dagens, Mayamata, 1970 (French)](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002); Sanskrit at [Mayamata chs 6–10, sa.wikisource](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/मयमतम्/अध्याय_०६-१०)
- RD. Mayamata 7.58 distributes the 81 squares differently from BS: "Dans le diagramme paramaśāyin, Kamalaja [Brahmā] occupe neuf cases, les quatre dieux qui lui font face six, ceux qui se tiennent aux points intermédiaires deux et tous ceux de la périphérie une" (9 + 4×6 + 8×2 + 32×1 = 81), against BS's 9 + 4×3 + 20×2 + 20×1 — [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002)
- RD. Mayamata 7.49 puts the Vāstupuruṣa's head to the **east** ("couché sur le sol, la tête vers l'Est"), not the north-east; Dagens's note: "Dans certains textes, il est dit que ce personnage est tourné vers le Nord-Est et qu'il occupe toute la surface du diagramme" — [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002)
- RD. Kramrisch: "The majority of texts place the Vāstupuruṣa with the head in the north-east" (BS LII.51, Matsya, etc.); head-east in a 64-square site, head north-east in an 81-square site (citing Samarāṅgaṇa XIV.11); "the two varieties intermingle" — [Kramrisch, The Hindu Temple vol. 1, pp. 78–79, 96](https://archive.org/details/hindu-temple-kramrisch)
- RD. Kramrisch on the corner deity: "Īśāna however does not appear in all the treatises as the regent of the north-east. Agni holds this position in most of the earlier texts"; note: "Agni (Śikhin) is the regent of the north-east according to Bṛhat Saṃhitā, LII. 43; Matsyapurāṇa, Kāmikāgama, Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra, Tantrasamuccaya, Viśvakarmavidyāprakāśa"; "Where Agni is stationed in the north-east, the south-east corner is the place of Vāyu" — [Kramrisch vol. 1, pp. 91–92](https://archive.org/details/hindu-temple-kramrisch)
- RD. Kramrisch on Āpa/Āpavatsa: "Filiation… associates Āpa, the Āditya of the north-east, with Āpavatsa. Āpa, in the Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra is identified with the Himālaya and Āpavatsa with his daughter Umā" — i.e. one major text does not read these two as "waters" at all — [Kramrisch vol. 1, p. 90](https://archive.org/details/hindu-temple-kramrisch)
- RD. Evidence that Āpa/Āpavatsa were nonetheless treated as aquatic: Mayamata 8.13–14 prescribes as their offering "pour Āpa et Āpavatsa du lotus, de la chair de [poisson], de coquillage et de tortue" (Skt *kumudaṃ matsyamāṃsaṃ ca śaṅkha-kacchapa-māṃsakam*) — [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002)
- RD. Agni Purāṇa 105.1–16 (Bibliotheca Indica text; Gangadharan tr.) has yet another allocation for the 81 squares (Īśa at NE, Havyavāha at SE, Varuṇa on the west; "(God) known as ĀYĀ [Āpa] in the two north-eastern squares of Brahmā. Apavatsa… in the six squares below that"), and adds a 100-square grid for a pavilion (105.14) — [Gangadharan tr., Agni Purāṇa ch. 105](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-agni-purana/d/doc1083335.html); [GRETIL Agni Purāṇa](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_agnipurANa.txt)
- RD. Shukla: the 45 gods' "number necessarily is the same in the Vāstupuruṣamaṇḍala of 64 or 81 or any of the other numbers of squares; only the extent allotted to each differs, but not their relative position in the plan" — [D. N. Shukla, Vāstu-śāstra vol. 1, ch. 6(ii), wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/vastu-shastra-indian-architecture/d/doc1085303.html)

#### 1d. Household functions explicitly assigned (by direction or by named square)

### Inferences
- The "water in the north-east" idea has a real anchor inside the grid — Āpa and Āpavatsa at the inner north-east, with Mayamata actually putting the water-store, basin and well on those squares — but in Varāhamihira's own grid the *corner* god of the north-east is Fire (Śikhin), and the "lord of the waters" (Ambupati/Jalādhipa) sits in the middle of the west in every list read.
- Latrines are absent from the Bṛhat Saṃhitā, Matsya, Agni and Garuḍa room lists; where one appears (Mayamata 26.214, Viśvakarmaprakāśa 2.96, Samarāṅgaṇa ch. 15) it is always in the south-west or south-south-west of a compound. A combined bath-plus-WC room is not a category any of these texts knows; they place the bath (east, Aditi/Uditi, Parjanya, or outside) and the latrine (south-west) in different places.
- The eight-room and sixteen-room lists are schemes for rooms ranged round a courtyard house or palace enclosure, not for subdividing one room.

- Dagens's English wording for Mayamata 26.214–216, 27.100–118 could not be read (archive.org OCR of the Motilal volumes is illegible; other hosts blocked). The translations above are mine and should be checked against Dagens before being quoted as his.

## 2. Water, wells and ground slope by direction: what the texts say and what they apply to

### Takeaway
The classical seed of "water in the north-east" is one verse of Varāhamihira (BS T 52.117 = K 53.119) on a body of water lying in each of the eight directions from the house — north gives wealth, north-east sons, every other direction harm — echoed in Viśvakarmaprakāśa 8.15–17 for reservoirs (wells, tanks), plus the Mayamata's well/cistern on the Īśa–Āpa–Āpavatsa squares; the seed of "slope to the north-east" is a set of site-selection verses on the fall (*plava*) of the plot. All of them speak of the plot, the well or the tank; none speaks of a bathing room, its floor or its drain, and the texts contradict one another (Mānasāra puts house wells in the south-east, several texts put water in Varuṇa's west, Mayamata allows reservoirs "wherever wanted").

### Inferences
- Every water verse read concerns a *source or store* of water for the house, village, temple or palace (well, tank, reservoir, cistern, drinking-water house), and every slope verse concerns the natural fall of the plot assessed before building. Extending them to the position of taps, a shower, a floor trap or the fall of a bathroom floor is a modern extrapolation with no textual statement behind it.
- The directional verdicts are not consistent across the corpus: north/north-east is good for water in BS and Viśvakarmaprakāśa; west is "best" or usable in Viśvakarmaprakāśa 7.115/8.15, Garuḍa and Samarāṅgaṇa but bad in BS; south-east is "fear of fire" in BS but is where Mānasāra XXXVI.27 puts house wells; Mayamata twice says "anywhere". A report should present "water in the north-east" as one textual preference among several, strongest for wells.
- For BS the caste-graded slope (north for Brahmins, east for Kṣatriyas, south for Vaiśyas, west for Śūdras) and for Mayamata/Mānasāra the north-or-east slope are the old forms; the single eight-direction verdict with north-east best appears in the later Viśvakarmaprakāśa.

### Gaps
- No eight-direction list of well results exists in the Matsya Purāṇa vāstu chapters (252–257) as digitised on sa.wikisource; the assignment's expectation of one there could not be confirmed and appears to be mistaken.
- A widely quoted well verse beginning *kūpe vāstor madhyadeśe…* (attributed in modern compilations to jyotiṣa *muhūrta* manuals) was not located in any source opened here; could not verify its text or locator.
- Bhaṭṭotpala's commentary on BS 52.117 (what exactly *salila* covers) was not read directly; only Bhat's gloss "tank, well, etc." was.
- The dating of the Viśvakarmaprakāśa and its relation to Varāhamihira's verse were not researched.

## 3. How zones are determined: the square grid, rectangular plots, the modern 22.5° method, and the worked example

### Takeaway
Classically a zone is a *pada*: the plot (or house enclosure, village, temple) is orientated by a gnomon to true east and divided by straight lines into a grid of 64 or 81 cells whose deities name the zones; the texts expressly foresee oblong sites (Mayamata 27.15–16 "whether oblong or square, divided into sixty-four"; sites longer by 1/10 to 1/4), and for a site twice as long as wide the Mayamata grids only a near-square portion rather than stretching the grid. No text read divides a house into equal compass angles from its centre; the sixteen-zone 22.5° wheel is a modern consultant device whose nearest classical relatives are the sixteen *perimeter* room positions of the Viśvakarmaprakāśa and an omen-wheel in the Bṛhat Saṃhitā. For the 28 × 67 ft example the three methods give three different answers — inner south-west (Indra/Jaya squares, clipping the Brahmā block) on a stretched grid; "between south and south-west" on the perimeter reading; west-south-west on the angular wheel — and the answer flips with a 2 ft change in the room's position.

#### 3d. Worked example (arithmetic; all results are my calculations)

Set-up. Plot 67 ft east–west by 28 ft north–south, assumed aligned to the cardinal directions. Origin at the plot centre, x positive east, y positive north; plot spans x −33.5…+33.5, y −14…+14. Room 6 × 6 ft with centre 12 ft west and 6 ft south: centre (−12, −6); room spans x −15…−9, y −9…−3. Checks against the description: west edge 18.5 ft and east edge 24.5 ft from the west boundary = 27.6 %–36.6 % of 67 ft (centre 32.1 %, "about one-third"); south face 5 ft from the south boundary (3 ft duct plus about 2 ft of walls).

1. **81-square grid stretched over the whole plot.** Cell = 67/9 = 7.444 ft (E–W) by 28/9 = 3.111 ft (N–S). Column boundaries from the west: −33.5, −26.06, −18.61, −11.17, −3.72, +3.72 …; row boundaries from the north: +14, +10.89, +7.78, +4.67, +1.56, −1.56, −4.67, −7.78, −10.89, −14. The centre (−12, −6) lies in column 3 (−18.61…−11.17) and row 7 (−4.67…−7.78): in the table of 1a that square is **Indra (Vibudhādhipa)**, the south-west member of the ring round Brahmā, diagonal neighbour of Jaya. The room's footprint splits 3.83 ft / 2.17 ft between columns 3 and 4 and 1.67 / 3.11 / 1.22 ft between rows 6, 7, 8, giving: Indra 11.93 sq ft (33.1 %), Vivasvān 6.74 (18.7 %), Mitra 6.39 (17.7 %), Bhṛṅgarāja's inner square 4.69 (13.0 %), **Brahmā's south-west square 3.61 (10.0 %)**, Gandharva's inner square 2.65 (7.4 %); total 36.0. The central nine squares span x ±11.17, y ±4.67, so the room's north-east corner (a strip 2.17 × 1.67 ft) falls inside the Brahmā block.
2. **64-square grid stretched** (cell 8.375 × 3.5 ft): centre in column 3, row 6 — the south-west corner square of the ring round Brahmā, which BS 52.55 gives half each to Indra and Jaya; footprint 58.3 % there, 33.3 % in the square south of it, 8.3 % in Mitra's square to the north; no overlap with Brahmā's four squares (x −8.375…+8.375, y −3.5…+3.5).
3. **Nine-zone reading** (3 × 3, cell 22.33 × 9.33 ft): centre in the south-west ninth (west third x < −11.17; south third y < −4.67); the same 3.61 sq ft corner lies in the central ninth.
4. **Perimeter "sixteen rooms" reading.** Sixteen positions = 4 corners + 4 mid-sides + 8 intermediates, i.e. five positions per side with corners shared; these are exactly the 16 border cells of a 5 × 5 grid. Along the south side in equal fifths of 13.4 ft: south-west corner 0–20 % from the west, **"between south and south-west" 20–40 %**, south 40–60 %, between south and south-east 60–80 %, south-east corner 80–100 %. The room occupies 27.6–36.6 %, wholly inside the second slot — the position Viśvakarmaprakāśa 2.96 gives to the *purīṣa-tyāga-mandira* (latrine). (Read strictly as a 5 × 5 grid, cell 13.4 × 5.6 ft, the room's centre is one cell in from the south border — row 4, column 2 — with only 10 % of it in the border cell, because of the duct.) On the 9 × 9 south border the same stretch is the Bhṛṅgarāja–Gandharva squares, between Yama (mid-south) and Pitṛ (south-west corner).
5. **Sixteen 22.5° sectors from the plot centre.** Bearing of the room centre clockwise from north = 180° + arctan(12/6) = 180° + 63.43° = **243.43°**. Sector limits: SSW 191.25–213.75°, SW 213.75–236.25°, **WSW 236.25–258.75°**, W 258.75–281.25°. The centre is in WSW. Corners: (−15,−9) 239.04° WSW; (−9,−9) 225.00° SW; (−15,−3) 258.69° WSW (0.06° inside the W boundary); (−9,−3) 251.57° WSW. Area split (numerical integration): 81.5 % WSW, 18.5 % SW. On an eight-sector (45°) wheel the centre is in SW (202.5–247.5°), 4° from the W boundary.
6. **Sensitivity.** If the room actually stands only 3 ft from the south boundary (centre 8 ft south, y −11…−5): on the stretched 81-grid the centre moves to row 8 (Bhṛṅgarāja's inner square; 33 % there, 30 % Indra, 19 % Gandharva's inner square, 17 % Vivasvān) and the overlap with Brahmā disappears; on the angular wheel the bearing becomes 180° + arctan(12/8) = 236.31°, 0.06° from the SW/WSW boundary, and the area splits 49.7 % SW / 50.3 % WSW. If the centre is taken at exactly one-third of the length from the west (x = −11.4), the Brahmā overlap grows to 4.61 sq ft (12.8 %). The angular result also changes if the consultant measures from the centre of the built envelope rather than of the plot, or if the plot is not square to true north.
7. **Mayamata's own treatment of a 2 : 1 site applied here** (the plot is 2.39 : 1, beyond anything the texts tabulate). *Tulānīya* rule (27.121): 67/7 = 9.571 ft per part; the middle three parts are 28.71 ft × 28 ft — almost exactly square — spanning x ±14.36; gridded 8 × 8 (cell 3.59 × 3.5 ft) the room centre is in the westernmost column, row 6 from the north (on the west border, third cell north of the south-west corner; I have not mapped the 64-grid deity for that cell), with 0.64 ft of the room outside the grid. *Kāyabhāra* rule (27.112–113): 67/5 = 13.4 ft; the western two parts (x < −6.7) are left out of the grid altogether, and the room lies wholly in that left-out strip.

### Inferences
- "Which zone is the room in" has no single classical answer for a plot this elongated: a stretched grid says inner south-west (Indra/Jaya, touching Mitra, Vivasvān and, marginally, the Brahmā block); the courtyard-perimeter list says "between south and south-west"; the Mayamata's own practice for long sites would either put it on the west edge of a central square grid or outside the grid; the modern wheel says WSW (or SW). A report should state the method before stating the zone.
- All classical readings nonetheless agree that the room is in the south-western part of the site, which is the one region the texts that mention a latrine give to it (Mayamata 26.214 Nirṛti; Viśvakarmaprakāśa 2.96 between Yama and Nirṛti; Samarāṅgaṇa ch. 15 Mṛga–Pitṛ). The texts that mention a *bath* put it elsewhere (east, Aditi/Uditi, Parjanya, or outside), so an attached bath-and-WC has only its WC half "classically located".
- On the stretched 81-grid a sliver (about 2.2 × 1.7 ft, 3.6 sq ft) of the room as described lies on the south-west Brahmā square; this depends on a 2 ft tolerance in the north–south position and on stretching the grid to a 2.39 : 1 plot, which no text prescribes. It is worth a measured check rather than a conclusion.
- The modern 22.5° wheel and a grid cannot agree on an elongated plan: equal angles from the centre give sectors of wildly unequal area in a 67 × 28 ft rectangle, whereas a grid gives equal cells. The wheel's "WSW" has no counterpart in any text read.
- The cells of a stretched 81-grid here are 7.4 × 3.1 ft; the texts' own oblong sites never exceed 1.25 : 1 for a gridded area (2 : 1 sites being cut down first), so a literal stretch is an extrapolation.

### Gaps
- Dagens's English for Mayamata 27.15–17 and 27.112–122 was not readable; the "oblong or square" and partial-gridding readings are my translations of clear Sanskrit and should be confirmed.
- Meister's JAOS 1979 and *Interdisciplinary Science Reviews* 1985 papers, Bafna's JSAH 2000 article and Chakrabarti's book were not opened (hosts blocked); only abstracts or Wikipedia's reports were used. Bafna's thesis that the maṇḍala was not a literal design grid is therefore not quoted.
- MahaVastu's own instructions for finding the centre and north (magnetic or true) were not read; the 22.5° figure is arithmetic from "16 zones", and the zone labels come from search-result summaries of MahaVastu-branded pages.
- The assumption that the plot is aligned to the cardinal directions, and the exact position of the room, need to be confirmed on a measured drawing; the sensitivity cases show the zone result is fragile.

## 4. Scope and scale: is the maṇḍala or the eight-guardian scheme ever applied inside a single room?

### Takeaway
The texts apply the deity grid to a site, a house compound, a village or town, a palace and a temple, and at the smallest scale to the temple sanctum (divided into seven for placing the image, with the liṅga nudged a barley-corn towards the north-east) and to the compartments of a foundation casket; they also give a handful of bodily-orientation rules (do not sleep with the head north or west; eat facing east; void facing north by day). Nothing comparable exists for a bathing room or latrine: the texts say where in the compound such a room goes, which way a person faces when voiding, and how far waste water must be kept from a neighbour, but give no rule for the position of anything inside it.

### Inferences
- The only recursive, room-scale use of zoning in the sources is sacral (sanctum, altar, foundation casket). Domestic interiors get rules about the body (which way the head, face or back points) and about a few named objects (hearth, mortar, water-store), all located on the grid of the whole house.
- For an attached bathroom the classical corpus therefore supports, at most, statements about (a) where in the house such a room lies and (b) which way the user of a WC faces (north by day, south by night per Manu, with a let-out for enclosed or dark places). Rules about which wall carries the tap, shower, basin, geyser, drain or mirror are not classical.

### Gaps
- No verse was found prescribing that the cook face east; it was looked for in BS 52, Matsya 252–257, Mayamata 27 and Viśvakarmaprakāśa ch. 2 only, so its absence from the wider literature is not established.
- The dharmaśāstra material on voiding (Āpastamba, Gautama, Yājñavalkya, Viṣṇu Smṛti parallels) was not collated; only Manu was read.
- Mayamata 27.88 (*na jātu svāmino vāse vaṃśānugataśāyanam*, apparently "never sleep along the vaṃśa in the master's room") was noted but not checked against a translation.

## 5. The centre: what the texts say about the Brahmasthāna in a house

### Takeaway
The texts ask that the central squares of Brahmā be "guarded": no pillar, wall, peg or nail on them, and no impure vessel, food-leavings, ash or buried foreign body, on pain of harm to the master of the house; in the Mayamata the centre of a house site is an open pavilion or court with an altar to Brahmā. The rule is about the central block of the whole house site (nine squares of 81, four of 64), not about the middle of each room.

### Cited Findings

- RD. BS T 52.64 = K 53.66: *sukham icchan brahmāṇaṃ yatnād rakṣed gṛhī gṛhāntaḥstham / ucchiṣṭādyupaghātād gṛhapatir upatapyate tasmin*. Bhat: "A house-owner wishing for happiness should guard very carefully Brahman (i.e. the nine squares occupied by Him) situated at the centre of the house. For, His affliction by dirty things like remnants of food, would harm the owner in it." Iyer: "…take particular care of the portion of the house which is the seat of Brahmā. If this portion be defiled by impurities, the master of the house will suffer miseries." — [GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_varAhamihira-bRhatsaMhitA.htm); [Bhat](https://archive.org/details/aqbw_brihat-samhita-part-1-by-varahamihira-translated-by-m-ramakrishna-bhat-sans); [Iyer](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brihat-samhita/d/doc229297.html)
- RD. - RD. Acharya's sceptical gloss: "The Bṛhatsaṃhitā has attempted an unscientific and mythological explanation. According to it the plots assigned to Brahmā and other superior deities should not be polluted by throwing remains of food and other refuse on them: this explanation, if it architecturally imply anything at all, would mean that certain plots of the ground-plan should be reserved as sacred places" — [Acharya, Architecture of Mānasāra, note on VII.266–269](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.108320)

### Inferences
- The classical prohibition names two kinds of thing for the centre: structural intrusions (pillar, wall, peg) and pollutants (*ucchiṣṭa*, impure vessels, ash, buried matter), plus a water body in one later text. A WC or drain on the central block would fall under the second kind by any reading; a bathroom elsewhere in the house is not addressed.
- Because the rule attaches to the central block of the house grid, whether the example room "touches the centre" depends entirely on the grid chosen (see 3d): it clips the Brahmā block by about 3.6 sq ft on a stretched 81-grid and not at all on a stretched 64-grid or if the room is 2 ft further south.

### Gaps
- Mayamata 27.96 (*brahmasthānaṃ talān nimnaṃ gṛhāntaḥ sthalam iṣyate*, apparently "the Brahmā-place lower than the floor, the house interior raised") was not checked against a translation; its meaning is uncertain and it is not relied on.
- Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra chapters on *marma-vedha* were not read directly; Kramrisch's chapter reference for the Indradhvaja note is partly illegible in the scan.

## 6. Mirrors: is there any classical rule for the wall or direction of a mirror?

### Takeaway
No. In every text searched the mirror (*darpaṇa*, *ādarśa*) is an auspicious emblem, a ritual implement, a toilet article or a piece of furniture with prescribed dimensions; nowhere is a wall or compass direction prescribed for hanging one in a room. The only mirrors that come with a direction are ritual ones: a small gold or silver mirror-emblem laid in a foundation-deposit casket (in the east in the Mayamata's Śiva-temple deposit) and a mirror among the toilet articles offered to Śiva's image in a temple rite (Agni Purāṇa 78.43–44, possibly "in the north"). No scholarly source was found tracing the modern "mirror on the north or east wall" rule to any text; it appears only in contemporary consultant and décor writing, alongside remedies openly labelled as borrowed from feng shui.

### Inferences
- The modern mirror rule is not derivable from any passage read: the texts never treat a mirror as something that modifies a room, only as a lucky object one sees, holds, gives or buries.
- Its likely pedigree is recent consultant literature: the reasoning offered (north = Kubera/wealth, east = sunrise, mirrors "double" what they face) and the company it keeps in remedy books (crystals, pyramids, explicitly feng-shui items) point to late-twentieth-century syncretism. This is circumstantial; no source was found that documents when or by whom the rule was first stated.
- The nearest thing to a classical "mirror direction" — the mirror-emblem at the east of a temple foundation casket (Mayamata 12.33–35) or fourth in a series beginning in the east (Mānasāra XII) — concerns a buried token and gives no support to a wall rule.

### Gaps
- No academic study of the history of the mirror rule (or of feng-shui borrowing into consultant Vāstu) was found; the origin remains undocumented.
- Not searched: Aparājitapṛcchā, Rājavallabha, Śilparatna, Manuṣyālayacandrikā, Bṛhadvāstumālā, Vāsturatnākara and other late compilations (no accessible e-texts; Sanskrit Wikisource search was rate-limited). A late regional manual containing such a rule cannot be excluded.
- The Samarāṅgaṇa and Mānasāra were searched in English translation (OCR), not in Sanskrit.

## 7. The guardians of the directions, Varuṇa in the west, and how the water element came to be put in the north-east

### Takeaway
The primary sources are unanimous on the eight guardians — Indra east, Agni south-east, Yama south, Nirṛti south-west, Varuṇa west, Vāyu north-west, Kubera (or Soma) north, Īśāna north-east — and the vāstu grids themselves seat the "lord of the waters" in the middle of the west. The modern "water = north-east" therefore does not come from the guardian scheme; its textual footholds are different: the Āpa/Āpavatsa squares at the inner north-east, the good omen of a well to the north or north-east, the north-east fall of the site, and one explicit five-element diagram in the Mayamata (the nine-square *pīṭha*: Water NE, Fire SE, Ether SW, Wind NW, Earth in the centre). No scholar's explanation of the modern five-element chart was found.

### Inferences
- The modern element chart (Water NE, Fire SE, Earth SW, Air NW, Space in the centre) coincides with the Mayamata's nine-square *pīṭha* at three corners and differs only by exchanging Earth and Ether between the south-west and the centre. It looks like a reworking of that minor diagram — one that Dagens himself sets apart from the Vāstupuruṣa grids — generalised to every house, reinforced by the Āpa/Āpavatsa names and the well verse. This is my inference; no source read states it.
- The south-east/Fire and north-west/Air pairs agree with the guardians (Agni, Vāyu), which makes the scheme look "classical"; the Water pair does not (Varuṇa is west), and in Varāhamihira's grid the north-east corner god is Fire. The element chart is thus not a restatement of the guardian scheme.
- For the question in hand: classical "north-east = water" is about where a *well, tank or cistern* lies in a compound and about deities' names; it is not a statement that bathing, plumbing or mirrors belong in the north-east, and the same texts place baths at Aditi/Uditi, in the east, or outside the house.

### Gaps
- No scholarly discussion explaining how or when the five elements were assigned to the corners in modern Vāstu was found; Chakrabarti's chapter on the maṇḍala (pp. 86–99) probably addresses the *pīṭha* but could not be opened.
- Whether other āgamic or śilpa texts (Kāmikāgama, Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati, Śilparatna) repeat the Mayamata's element diagram was not checked.
- The earlier history of the guardians (Vedic lokapālas, the Soma/Kubera alternation in the north) was taken only from Kramrisch and Manu 5.96.
