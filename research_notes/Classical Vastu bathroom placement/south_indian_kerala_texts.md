# South Indian (Maya / Drāviḍa) and Kerala vāstu texts and living house traditions: where the bath, the latrine, wells and water points belong

Evidence tags used in every finding: **[RD]** = read directly (I opened the passage in the edition or e-text named; most scans were read through archive.org OCR text, so single letters can be wrong and doubtful readings are flagged). **[RB: name]** = reported by a named secondary source that I read. **[CNV]** = could not verify. English in quotation marks followed by "(mine)" is my own rendering of Sanskrit that I read; all other quoted English is verbatim from the translator named. Researched November 2026.

Plot-square (pada) names used below. Mayamata 7.33b-39 lists the 32 outer deities clockwise from the north-east corner: Īśa (NE corner), Parjanya, Jayanta, Mahendra, Āditya, Satyaka, Bhṛśa, Antarikṣa, Agni (SE corner), Pūṣan, Vitatha, Rākṣasa (= Gṛhakṣata), Yama, Gandharva, Bhṛṅgarāja, Mṛṣa, Pitṛ (= Nirṛti, SW corner), Dauvārika, Sugrīva, Puṣpadanta, Jalādhipa (= Varuṇa), Asura, Śoṣa, Roga, Vāyu (NW corner), Nāga, Mukhya, Bhallāṭa, Soma, Mṛga, Aditi, Uditi; Āpa and Āpavatsa are the inner squares at the NE corner [RD, Dagens's French translation] — [Dagens 1970, Mayamata I, ch. VII](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002). Kerala naming differs: Manuṣyālaya Candrikā 2.24-25 calls the square before Pitṛ "Mṛga", the square after Soma "Argala", the last two northern squares "Aditi, Diti", and the inner SW square "Indrajit" [RD] — [MC e-text](https://archive.org/details/sthapatyavedmanushyalayacandrika).

## 1. Mayamata: in the chapters on houses and palaces, where are the bath, latrine, water store and well placed?

### Takeaway
In Dagens's text the general house-compound rule (26.214-216) puts the latrine (*varcas*) together with the lying-in room at Nirṛti (south-west), the shrine and the well (*kūpa*) at Īśa (north-east) and the bath (*snāna*) at Uditi, next to the north-east corner; chapter 27 adds a water store (*jalakośa*) at Āpavatsa, a basin (*kuṇḍa*) at Āpa and a well at Parjanya, and the palace chapter keeps bath-houses, drinking-water and hot-water rooms, tanks and wells in the north-east sector, with one doubtful verse (29.117) that may put a latrine beside the well at Īśa. I could read Dagens's Sanskrit and verse numbers but not his English wording, so the translations here are mine.

#### Chapter 26 (śālāvidhāna, houses): the one general statement
- Mayamata 26.214 (Dagens; = TSS 26.207) [RD]: *prathamāvaraṇe dvitīyake vā parabhāge sadanaṃ tu yoṣitām | nirṛtau sūtigṛhaṃ ca varcasaḥ pavanādīnduṣu vāṅganāgṛham* (TSS reads *pavanādīndudikṣu*). "In the first enclosure or the second, in the rear part, is the women's residence; at Nirṛti [SW] the lying-in room (*sūti-gṛha*) and that of excrement (*varcas*, the latrine); or the women's house lies from Pavana [NW] to Indu [N]." (mine) — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, Sanskrit p. 570](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das); [TSS 65](https://archive.org/details/TSS065MayamatamTGSastri1919_201809).
- The Hindi rendering construes 26.214 the same way: "in the south-west direction should be the lying-in room and the latrine (*śaucālaya*)" [RD] — [Hindi rendering](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%80_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%96%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE).
- Mayamata 26.215 [RD]: *yamadiśi bhojanaśālā some dhanasañcayāvāsam | agnau dhānyāgāraṃ khe paktir vyañjanāni tatraiva*. Dining hall in the south; treasury at Soma [N]; granary at Agni [SE]; cooking (*pakti*) and condiments at Kha [the Antarikṣa square, east side next to the SE corner] (mine) — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- Mayamata 26.216 (= TSS 26.209) [RD]: *ārādhanagṛham īśe kūpaṃ tatroditau snānam | yasmin yasminn uditāny anyāni hi tatra saṃprayojyāni*. "The worship room at Īśa [NE], the well (*kūpa*) there; the bath (*snāna*) at Uditi; the other things are to be put wherever they have been prescribed." (mine) — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Edition conflict at 26.216: Dagens's apparatus records the variant *tatroditaṃ* (manuscripts B, C) for *tatroditau*; with the variant the sense is "the bath is prescribed there [at Īśa]" rather than "the bath at Uditi". The Hindi rendering follows *uditau*: "the bathing place should be on the square of Uditi" [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, apparatus n. 353](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das); [Hindi rendering](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%80_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%96%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE).
- Mayamata 26.218 [RD]: cattle shed (*goṣṭha*) at Śikhin [SE], goats and sheep in the south-west, buffaloes in the north-west, horse and elephant stables at the NE corner, vehicles to the left of the door. 26.219 says the scheme is "common to the four [classes]" (*sāmānyaṃ syād etad uktaṃ caturṇām*), with special provisions for kings — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- A keyword search of the Sanskrit of chapter 26 for *varcas, snāna, majjana, kūpa, vāpī, jala, udaka, mūtra* finds only 26.214 and 26.216; chapter 28 (first entry into the house) has no placement rule [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).

#### Chapter 27 (caturgṛhavidhāna, four-building house for the four classes)
- Water outlets (*vāriniḥsrava*) are fixed with each main building's door: 27.45 door at Mahendra, outlet at Mukhya (Brahmin's northern house); 27.48 door at Gṛhakṣata, outlet at Jayanta (king's eastern house); 27.51 door at Puṣpadanta, outlet at Vitatha (Vaiśya's southern house); 27.54 door at Bhallāṭa, outlet at Sugrīva (Śūdra's western house) [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Mayamata 27.100 [RD]: *kāñjīlavaṇayoḥ pātre prāgudagdiśi vinyaset | antarikṣe bhavec cullī satyake syād ulūkhalam | aiśānyāṃ pacanasthānaṃ sarveṣāṃ dehināṃ hitam*. Vessels of sour gruel and salt in the north-east; hearth at Antarikṣa; mortar at Satyaka; "a cooking place in the north-east is good for all" (mine) — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Mayamata 27.107-108 [RD]: *sugale puṣpadante ca prasūtigṛham iṣyate || jalakośam āpavatse kuṇḍam āpe vidhīyate*. The childbirth room (*prasūti-gṛha*) at Sugala [Sugrīva] and Puṣpadanta [west]; "the water store (*jala-kośa*) at Āpavatsa, the basin (*kuṇḍa*) at Āpa" (mine). The Hindi rendering has "water store at Āpavatsa and water basin (*jalakuṇḍa*) at Āpa" — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6); [Hindi rendering](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%80_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%96%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE).
- Mayamata 27.114-118 (the *kāyabhāra* layout "for wealthy Vaiśyas", 27.119) [RD]: the *ariṣṭāgāra* (lying-in chamber) on Bhṛṅgarāja, Dauvārika, Sugrīva and Pitṛ [the SW sector]; *vāhanaṃ dvāravāme ca dānaśālā ca vāruṇe* (vehicles left of the door, alms hall at Varuṇa [W]); then *jayante cāpavatse ca parjanye ca śive kramāt | viṣaṃ ca viṣaghātaṃ ca kūpaṃ devagṛhaṃ bhavet*: poisons at Jayanta, antidotes at Āpavatsa, the well (*kūpa*) at Parjanya, the shrine at Śiva [Īśa] (mine) — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Edition conflict at 27.115: Dagens prints *dānaśālā ca vāruṇe* but records manuscript B as reading *snānaśālā* (bath hall at Varuṇa); the parallel Kāmikāgama verse reads *snānaśālā ca vāruṇe* (see below) [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, apparatus n. 166](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Chapter 27 contains no word for latrine (*varcas, mūtra, purīṣa, avaskara, śauca*) in the OCR text [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).

#### Chapter 29 (rājaveśmavidhāna, palaces)
- First enclosure, 29.33-34 [RD]: *bhṛṅgarāje 'śvaśālā syād mṛṣe cārtavasūtikā*: horse stable at Bhṛṅgarāja; at Mṛṣa [south side, next to the SW corner] the room for menstruation and childbirth (*ārtava-sūtikā*); water-play (*jalalīlā*) at Dauvārika and Sukaṇṭha [Sugrīva]. Dagens's apparatus gives the variant *vṛṣe* for *mṛṣe* — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, Sanskrit p. 630](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- First enclosure, 29.39-40 [RD]: *abhito majjanāgāram udityām āpavatsayoḥ || pānīyoṣṇodakaṃ dhāma …*. "Nearby, the bath-house (*majjanāgāra*) at Uditi and on the two Āpa-Āpavatsa squares; [with] a room for drinking water and hot water (*pānīya-uṣṇodaka*)." (mine). The Hindi rendering: "the bath-house should be on the squares of Uditi and Āpavatsa, where there should be drinking water and hot water" — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, Sanskrit p. 632](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das); [Hindi rendering](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%80_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%96%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE).
- Edition conflict at 29.39: *udityām* ("at Uditi") is Dagens's correction; his apparatus says manuscripts A and B read *udīcyām* ("in the north") [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, apparatus n. 64](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Second enclosure, 29.50 [RD]: *nirṛtau māhiṣaṃ gehaṃ dauvārikasukaṇṭhayoḥ | dānaśālekṣaṇaśālā snānaṃ puṣpādike gṛhe*: buffalo house at Nirṛti; alms hall and viewing hall at Dauvārika and Sukaṇṭha; "the bath in a building at Puṣpa[danta] and the next squares" [west] (mine). The words *snānaṃ puṣpādike* are Dagens's correction of manuscripts that read *snānapīṭhādike* — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, Sanskrit p. 636, apparatus n. 81](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Second enclosure, 29.51 and 29.53-54 [RD]: a long pond (*dīrghikā*) at Nāga and Rudra [NW]; *āpe caivāpavatse ca vāpī kūpaṃ ca dīrghikā | pānīyasadanaṃ puṣpodyānaṃ tatraiva kīrtitam*: "at Āpa and Āpavatsa a tank (*vāpī*), a well (*kūpa*) and a long pond; the drinking-water house (*pānīya-sadana*) and flower garden are there too" (mine) — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- Third enclosure, 29.62-63 and 29.66-67 [RD]: a tank at Vāyu [NW] and a lotus pond (*puṣkariṇī*) at Nāga; a childbirth room (*prasūtikā*) at Soma [N]; long ponds at Mṛga, Aditi, Uditi, Īśāna and Jayanta; and, for the surrounding town, *sarvatra dīrghikārāmavāpīkūpasamanvitam* ("furnished everywhere with ponds, gardens, tanks and wells") — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- Named palace types: 29.101 *prāguttaradiśi salilaṃ snānaṃ devālayaṃ tu homāgram* (water, bath, shrine and fire-offering place in the north-east); 29.133 *tatprāguttarakoṇe majjanaśālā ca devagṛham* (bathing hall and shrine at the NE corner); 29.137 *dvārasyottarapārśve pānīyoṣṇodakālayaṃ kuryāt* (drinking-water and hot-water room on the north side of the door); 29.145 *abhitaḥ snānaṃ ca maṇḍapaṃ ca syāt* (a bath and a pavilion beside the hall); 29.150 water-sport, a "water house" (*salilāvāsa*) and the king's long pond in the northern part; 29.151-152 the chosen deity's shrine, flower gardens and a well outside to the north-east [RD] — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- The doubtful latrine verse, 29.115-117 (second enclosure of the *Saubala* palace) [RD]: *śoṣe cāntaḥpuraṃ vāyau vāpīkrīḍāsamanvitam … tatraiva hastiśālā syād īśe vāpī ca kūpakam | vacorgehaṃ tu tatraiva jalayantraṃ vidhīyate*. A tank with water-sport at Vāyu [NW]; a tank and a well at Īśa [NE]; "the *vacor-geha* is there too, and a water machine (*jala-yantra*) is prescribed" (mine) — [Dagens ed. vol. 2, Sanskrit p. 650](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Edition conflict at 29.117: Dagens's apparatus (n. 152) says the printed word is a correction and that manuscripts A, B, E read *vaco-*; the 1919 edition (its 29.119) prints *vacogehaṃ(?)* with the editor's own question mark; the Hindi rendering takes it as "*varcogṛha* (latrine)" placed at Īśa with the water machine. The reading "latrine" therefore rests on an emendation, and it would put a latrine at the north-east, against 26.214 [RD] — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das); [TSS 65](https://archive.org/details/TSS065MayamatamTGSastri1919_201809); [Hindi rendering](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%80_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%96%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE).

#### Site chapters (villages, temple enclosures) and the appendix on wells
- Mayamata 9.87-88 [RD]: cattle shed in the south, flower garden in the north; *sarvatraiva jalāśayam iṣṭaṃ vāpī ca kūpaś ca* — "a reservoir, a tank and a well are wanted everywhere". Dagens's French: "Là où l'on désire de l'eau, il faut disposer un bassin ou un réservoir" — [Dagens ed. vol. 1](https://archive.org/details/GwHQ_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-1-motilal-banarasi-das); [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002).
- Mayamata 9.96-98 [RD]: *caṇḍālakuṭīrāṇi pūrvāyāṃ krośamātre tu … pūrvāhne malamokṣakriyocitā grāmam āveśya … prāguttaradiśi daṇḍaiḥ pañcaśate syāc chavāvāsam*. Caṇḍāla huts one *krośa* to the east; they enter the village in the forenoon for "the work of removing filth" (*mala-mokṣa-kriyā*); the cremation ground 500 *daṇḍa*s to the north-east. Dagens's French: "en entrant au village, le matin, les Caṇḍāla doivent le débarrasser des ordures" — [Dagens ed. vol. 1](https://archive.org/details/GwHQ_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-1-motilal-banarasi-das); [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002).
- Mayamata 9.99-100 [RD]: misplacing the Caṇḍāla quarter, tanners, cremation ground or water reservoirs (*toyāśaya*), letting refuse pile up (*śodhya-sañcaya*) and throwing impurities in the street (*mārge 'śuddha-kṣepaṇam*) bring ruin to the village. Dagens's French: "s'il y a des monceaux d'ordures et si l'on jette des saletés dans les rues, cela cause l'anéantissement du village" — [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002).
- Temple enclosures, Mayamata 23.84-86 [RD]: *yāmye majjanaśālā syāt tatrasthaṃ puṣpamaṇḍapam … īśe vāpī ca kūpaṃ syād āpavatsapadāśritam*. ### Inferences
- The Mayamata's house rule has a clear diagonal: water, worship and bathing at the north-east corner group (Īśa, Uditi, Parjanya, Āpa, Āpavatsa), birth-impurity and excrement at the south-west corner group (Nirṛti, Mṛṣa, Bhṛṅgarāja, Dauvārika, Sugrīva).
- These are functions spread over a compound of several buildings and enclosures (the verse itself opens "in the first enclosure or the second"), not rooms inside one building; the text never speaks of a latrine attached to a living room.
- Only one verse in the house chapters names a latrine at all (26.214). The second candidate (29.117) is too uncertain to build on.
- The western bath (*snānaśālā* at Varuṇa in the Kāmika and in one Mayamata manuscript; bath at Puṣpadanta in 29.50) is a second, weaker tradition beside the north-east bath; it sits next to the Kāmika's "water room in the west".
- If *maitra-sthāna* in Kāmika 35.180 means "place for evacuation" (see section 4), the Kāmika and the Mayamata agree on a south-west latrine; Sabharathnam's "reception hall" would remove that agreement.

- V. Ganapati Sthapati's books were not located; nothing is reported from them.

## 2. Mānasāra: what do the chapter on the arrangement of rooms and the palace chapter say about bath, latrine and water?

### Takeaway
Acharya's Mānasāra puts the bath of "all castes" at Aditi (36.41) and the palace bath-house (*majjanālaya*) at Aditi or Udita (40.103), kitchens in the north, north-east or Parjanya (36.26), and — unusually — wells at Antarikṣa, Agni and Pūṣan, the south-east sector (36.27); neither chapter mentions a latrine in any form.

### Inferences
- Mānasāra and Mayamata agree on the bath at the Aditi/Uditi squares, the north side beside the north-east corner; this is the best-attested Drāviḍa position for the bathing place.
- Mānasāra 36.27 (wells in the south-east sector) contradicts the Mayamata's north-east wells, the Mayamata appendix and the Manuṣyālaya Candrikā (a south-east well brings fire and fear). It should be reported as an outlier within the tradition, not harmonised away; Acharya himself warns of the "defective nature of the text".
- *Sūtakārālaya* at Yama or Nirṛti (36.43) may be a corrupt "lying-in room" (*sūtikāgāra*) rather than "charioteers' houses", given the Mayamata's *sūtigṛha* at Nirṛti and Mānasāra 40.105; this is my conjecture, not Acharya's reading.
- The "pavilion for cleansing the body" outside the palace bath-house (40.103-104) is the nearest thing to a sanitary annexe in the Mānasāra; nothing shows it was a latrine.

## 3. Manuṣyālaya Candrikā (and the other Kerala texts): what is prescribed for the well, tank, bathing place, latrine and cowshed?

### Takeaway
The Manuṣyālaya Candrikā (ch. 7) gives detailed positions for the well (best at Mīna, the north-east corner sign), the tank, the kitchen and the cowshed, says bathing water and drinking water should normally be separate, places a bath only in the royal house (at Parjanya), and has no verse on a latrine at all. The Kerala Vāstuvidyā, the Śilparatna and the Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati are equally silent on latrines; the Tantrasamuccaya does not deal with houses.

### Inferences
- All the Kerala texts treat water as a north-east matter (well at Mīna or the Īśa quarter, bath at Parjanya, tank east or west) and say nothing of a latrine; in this tradition the latrine is simply not part of the house the texts design.
- The bath itself is a royal provision in these texts (MC 7.34, Śilparatna 5.88-90, ĪŚGDP 25.81). For an ordinary house the MC supplies a tank and the rule of separate bathing and drinking water, which fits bathing at a tank rather than in a room.
- The MC's admission that tanks are "also seen" in the north-west and south-west reads like a concession to existing practice (compare the south-west tanks in section 6).
- The zodiac rankings of well positions disagree between texts (Mīna best in the MC; Kumbha best in the ĪŚGDP and the "Kūpapraśna"); a consultant citing "the Kerala texts" for one ranking is choosing a text.

### Gaps
- Achyuthan and Prabhu's English translation and *A Text Book of Vāstuvidyā* could not be accessed; P. Ramakrishnan's thesis (CUSAT 1998), said to contain the text with an English translation, sits on a blocked host.
- The 1917 Trivandrum edition of the MC (TSS 56) has no usable OCR; the 1928 Kochi edition's reading of the tank verse is unverified.
- The "Pañcāśikā" and "Kūpapraśna" are known to me only through the 1940 commentator's quotations.

## 4. Do any of these texts place the latrine outside the house or compound, and where? At what distance?

### Takeaway
No vāstu text read here gives a distance for a household latrine. The Mayamata puts it at the south-west of the compound's enclosures; the Mānasāra, the Manuṣyālaya Candrikā and the other Kerala texts name none; the rule "go away from the house" comes from the ritual-conduct chapters of the Kāmikāgama and from the dharma texts, which give no measure either. The only measured distances in the vāstu texts are for the scavenger caste's hamlet and the cremation ground.

### Cited Findings
- Mayamata 26.214 [RD]: the latrine (*varcas*) at Nirṛti is listed in a passage that opens "in the first enclosure or the second" (*prathamāvaraṇe dvitīyake vā*) and is declared common to all four classes (26.219); no distance is stated — [Dagens ed. vol. 2](https://archive.org/details/Xveg_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-2-motilal-banarasi-das).
- Mayamata 9.96-98 [RD]: the distances given are for the Caṇḍāla huts (one *krośa* east of the village) and the cremation ground (500 *daṇḍa*s north-east); the Caṇḍālas come in each forenoon to remove filth (*mala-mokṣa*) — [Dagens ed. vol. 1](https://archive.org/details/GwHQ_mayamata-by-bruno-dagens-vol.-1-motilal-banarasi-das); [Dagens 1970](https://archive.org/details/mayamatabronodagens_202002).
- ĪŚGDP 25.77, Śilparatna 5.74 and Kāśyapa 43.92 repeat the same kind of rule: Caṇḍāla hamlet at one or two *krośa*s, cremation ground a *krośa* to the north-east [RD] — [ĪŚGDP vol. 3](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.345368); [Śilparatna, Part I](https://archive.org/details/TSS075TheSilparatnaOfSriKumaraKSambasivaSastriVolume11922_201809); [Kāśyapa Śilpaśāstra e-text](https://archive.org/details/sthapatyvedkashyapashilpashastra).
- Mānasāra 36 and 40, MC, Vāstuvidyā 4, Śilparatna I.5, ĪŚGDP 23-25: no latrine and so no distance (see sections 2 and 3) [RD] — [sa.wikisource Mānasāram](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D); [MC e-text](https://archive.org/details/sthapatyavedmanushyalayacandrika).
- Kāmikāgama, Pūrvabhāga 3.3-4 (daily conduct) [RD]: the practitioner rises before dawn and *gatvā niketanād bāhye vijanaṃ sajalāśayam* — Sabharathnam: "he should come out of his house and proceed towards a water-source … while it is not frequented by the people. There … attend to the call of nature and take bath." Places to avoid include paths, grass, rivers, cow-pens, gardens and crossroads (3.5-7) — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).
- Kāmikāgama 3.17-18 [RD]: *gṛhe maitrapade vāpi kṛtvā cāvaśyakakriyām | śaucaṃ samācaret snānaṃ mandire prāgvidhānataḥ* — Sabharathnam: "Attending to the call of nature and bath may be done even in one's own house, especially in a separate room designed in the Mitra pada (segment belonging to the vastu Deity, Mitra) of the house." — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).
- Kāmikāgama 35.180 [RD]: *maitrasthānaṃ tu vā tatra* follows "armoury in the south-west"; Sabharathnam renders it "Or, reception hall may be in the south-west" — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).
- In Manu's usage *maitra* is the act of evacuation: Manu 4.152 *maitraṃ prasādhanaṃ snānaṃ dantadhāvanam añjanam | pūrvāhṇa eva kurvīta*; and Manu 4.151 gives the general rule *dūrād āvasathān mūtraṃ … dūrād eva samācaret* ("far from the dwelling") [RD] — [Manusmṛti, GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_manusmRti.txt).
- Āpastamba Dharmasūtra 1.11.31.3 [RD]: *astam ite ca bahir grāmād ārād āvasathād vā mūtrapurīṣayoḥ karma varjayet* — after sunset one should avoid voiding urine and faeces outside the village or far from the dwelling (so by day that is where it is done) — [Āpastamba Dharmasūtra, GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_ApastambadharmasUtra.txt).
- Mānasāra 9.158 [RD]: *tasmād dūre tu deśe ca caṇḍālakuṭikānvitam* — the Caṇḍāla huts "at a distance" beyond the village (Acharya renders "the cottage of the undertakers") — [sa.wikisource Mānasāram](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D); [Acharya 1934, ch. IX](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.108320).

### Inferences
- The textual picture is consistent: excretion belongs outdoors, away from the dwelling, with waste removal as the task of a service caste; a built latrine appears once (Mayamata 26.214), at the south-west edge of a large compound, and possibly a second time as the Kāmika's *maitra*-place.
- Since *maitra-pada* in Kāmika 3.17 is where "the necessary act" is done at home, *maitra-sthāna* in 35.180 most naturally means the same place, located with the armoury in the south-west. Sabharathnam's two renderings ("Mitra pada" in 3.17, "reception hall" in 35.180) are not consistent with each other; this reading is mine and should be labelled as such.
- A statement such as "the texts require the latrine N metres from the house" has no basis in the texts read here.

### Gaps
- No commentary on Kāmikāgama 3.17 or 35.180 was available to settle *maitra-pada / maitra-sthāna* (place of Mitra's square, in the west, or place of evacuation).
- The Ajitāgama and other āgamas with house or conduct chapters were not checked.

## 5. Do any of them say which direction a person should face while bathing or relieving themselves, give rules for the inside of a bathing room, or give rules for mirrors?

### Takeaway
Nothing of the kind is in the vāstu texts read (Mayamata 26-29, Mānasāra 36 and 40, Manuṣyālaya Candrikā, Vāstuvidyā 4, Śilparatna I.5, ĪŚGDP 23-25): no facing direction for bathing or for the latrine, no rule for the interior of a bathing room, no rule for where a mirror goes. A facing rule for excretion exists only as a rule of personal purity for open-air use in the Kāmikāgama's conduct chapter and in the dharma texts (north by day, south by night); the mirror appears in the Mānasāra only as an object with prescribed dimensions.

### Cited Findings
- Negative result, Mayamata [RD]: the only bath and latrine statements in chapters 26-29 are positional (26.214, 26.216, 29.39-40, 29.50, 29.101, 29.133, 29.137, 29.145, 29.117); none mentions a facing direction, a fitting, a seat, a drain inside the room or a mirror; the word *darpaṇa* does not occur in chapters 26-30 — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6).
- Negative result, Manuṣyālaya Candrikā [RD]: no *darpaṇa / mukura*, no facing rule; the only statements about bathing are 7.29 (separate water for bathing and drinking) and 7.34 (royal bath at Parjanya) — [MC e-text](https://archive.org/details/sthapatyavedmanushyalayacandrika).
- Negative result, Mānasāra 36 and 40 [RD]: positions only (36.41, 36.54, 40.103-104) — [Mānasāra ch. 36, wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/manasara-english-translation/d/doc421084.html); [Mānasāra ch. 40, wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/manasara-english-translation/d/doc421088.html).
- Mirrors in the Mānasāra are furniture, not a placement rule: 50.111-132 give the mirror's width ("from five or six aṅgulas … [to] twenty-one or twenty-two"), its round form, rim, painted back and handle; 50.165 fixes mirrors on the porch of a swing [RD] — [Acharya 1934, ch. L](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.108320).
- What the texts do say about bath annexes [RD]: a drinking-water and hot-water room goes with the palace bath-house (Mayamata 29.39-40, 29.137); an oil-anointing pavilion and a body-cleansing pavilion stand outside the bath (Mānasāra 36.54, 40.104) — [sa.wikisource chs 26-30](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF_%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%AC-%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6); [Mānasāra ch. 40, wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/manasara-english-translation/d/doc421088.html).
- Facing while relieving oneself, Kāmikāgama 3.8-9 [RD]: *divā vā sandhyayoḥ saumyavaktraḥ … rātrau dakṣiṇavaktras tu tṛṇādyantarite bhuvi* — Sabharathnam: "he should sit facing the north in the day-time and during the time of twilight (sandhya) and pass out urine and feces. He should sit facing the south, in the night time. He should do these two activities in a spot covered by dry grass and such others and well enclosed by bushes". Verse 3.7 forbids facing the sun, moon or fire — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).
- The same rule in Manu 4.50 [RD]: *mūtroccārasamutsargaṃ divā kuryād udaṅmukhaḥ | dakṣiṇābhimukho rātrau saṃdhyāyoś ca yathā divā* — [Manusmṛti, GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_manusmRti.txt).
- Āpastamba Dharmasūtra 1.11.31.1 [RD]: *prāṅmukho 'nnāni bhuñjīta, uccared dakṣiṇāmukhaḥ* (eat facing east; void facing south); 1.11.30.20 forbids facing fire, the sun, water, a Brahmin, cows or deities — a different facing rule from Manu's — [Āpastamba Dharmasūtra, GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_ApastambadharmasUtra.txt).
- Bathing at home, Kāmikāgama 3.40-41 [RD]: *athavā svagṛhe śuddhavāriṇā śītalena vā taptena liptabhūmisthapīṭhasthaḥ snānam ācaret* — Sabharathnam: "the daily bath may be taken in one's own house making use of pure water, either cold or hot. Being seated on the pedestal placed on the ground which is well smeared with the cow-dung … with the water kept in 9, 8 or 5 vessels". No direction to face and no room are specified — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).
- Ranking of bathing waters, Kāmikāgama 3.23-24 [RD]: rivers, tanks and confluences are best, a lotus pool middling, a long pond inferior, *kūpe caivādhamādhamam* — "Taking bath in a well is of lowermost nature." — [Kāmika Āgama, tr. Sabharathnam](https://archive.org/details/kamika-agama-purva-pada-part-1).

### Inferences
- Modern prescriptions about the direction a WC pan should face, where the shower, tap, geyser or mirror should be inside a bathroom, or mirrors on particular walls, cannot be derived from these texts: the texts stop at "which square of the compound".
- The only authentic facing rule (north by day, south by night; or Āpastamba's south) is a purity rule for a person squatting outdoors; transferring it to the orientation of a fixed toilet is a modern step, and the two old sources do not even agree with each other.

### Gaps
- Second parts of the Śilparatna and the ĪŚGDP's other pādas were not searched for conduct rules; the search covered the architectural chapters named above.

## 6. How did traditional Kerala and Tamil houses actually handle bathing and latrines?

### Takeaway
In Kerala the Nair and Nambudiri compound bathed in its own tank (*kuḷam*) with a covered bathing shed (*kuḷappura*), drew drinking and cooking water from a well attached to the kitchen at the north-east of the house, and had either no latrine or a detached screened shed (*marappura*) at a distance, recorded in the south-west quarter of the compound. In Tamil houses bathing was at a tank, canal or well or in a screened corner of the back yard, and latrines, where they existed, were at the far rear of the plot and emptied by scavengers from a back door; a government gazetteer still noted "no latrines" in most houses of one district in 1972.

### Inferences
- Kerala practice matches the Kerala texts closely on water (house in the north-east quarter, kitchen and well at its north-east, tank near the well) and fills the texts' silence on the latrine with a detached shed far from the house, on the record in the south-west quarter beside the cowshed; the cremation plot and the unbuilt *Vāyu* quarter show that the compound, not the house, is the unit being zoned.
- Tank positions vary in the ethnographies (north-east of the well; south-west beside the serpent grove; "north-east or south-west"), which is exactly the range the Manuṣyālaya Candrikā allows ("also seen at Vāyu or Nirṛti").
- The Nambudiri rule that wells are not for bathing, and the Kāmikāgama's ranking of a well-bath as the lowest, explain why these traditions provide a tank and a bathing shed rather than a bathing room.
- In Tamil row houses the latrine's position follows the plot: it is at the back, where the scavenger's lane is, whichever compass direction that happens to be; Reiniche's informants gave directional rules for the door, lamp, hearth and the south-west corner, not for the latrine.
- The Tirunelveli use of the south-west corner for birth, puberty seclusion and the dead parallels the Mayamata's lying-in room at Nirṛti; this is a correspondence I observe, not one the sources draw.

### Gaps
- Ashalatha Thampuran's *Traditional Architectural Forms of Malabar Coast*, Henri Schildt's *The Traditional Kerala Manor* and Melinda Moore's "The Kerala House as a Hindu Cosmos" could not be accessed; nothing is reported from them.
- No measured survey of a Chettinad house showing its original sanitary arrangements was found; the gazetteer is the only source used, and it describes the 1960s-70s.
- The page of the gazetteer's dysentery remark could not be read off the OCR. Menon's own English gloss for *marappura* is missing in the scan; "latrine building" is Bernier's paraphrase.
- For Thanjavur agrahāram houses (Béteille, Gough) only search snippets were obtainable, so they are not cited.

## 7. Where do the south Indian texts agree or conflict with the north Indian "sixteen-room" scheme (bath in the east, latrine between south and south-west)?

### Takeaway
They agree on the south-west as the zone of impurity (latrine and lying-in room) and on the north-east as the zone of worship and water, but they do not put the bath due east: the Drāviḍa texts put it at Aditi/Uditi (north side by the north-east corner), the Kerala texts at Parjanya (east side by the north-east corner), and one strand in the west. They also allow the kitchen in the north-east, which the sixteen-room scheme reserves for the shrine, and they work by named squares of a compound, not by sixteen rooms round a courtyard.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- "Bath in the east" is the northern formula. The defensible southern statement is "bath beside the north-east corner" — north side in the Mayamata and Mānasāra, east side in the Kerala texts and the Kāśyapa — with a western alternative; a report should not credit the Mayamata or the Manuṣyālaya Candrikā with "bath in the east".
- "Latrine between south and south-west" is northern wording; the only southern verse says "at Nirṛti" (south-west), and the Kerala texts say nothing. Claims that the southern texts prescribe north-west or west toilets, forbid them in particular rooms, or regulate attached bathrooms have no support in the passages read.
- The Kāmika's eight-direction list (35.179-180) is close kin to the Agni Purāṇa and *Vāstu-pravandha* lists, so some northern-looking compass schemes do occur in southern sources; but even there the bath is missing and water is in the west.
- Rules for septic tanks, attached toilets, overhead tanks, bore-wells and geysers are modern extensions by analogy (the Kannada commentary on MC 7.28 openly adds bore-wells and underground tanks to the north-east well rule); they are commentary, not text.

### Gaps
- The northern texts themselves were read only in Acharya's quotations; their own chapter contexts are another researcher's scope.
- Vibhuti Chakrabarti's *Indian Architectural Theory*, which tabulates room positions across texts, could not be accessed.
- I did not find any southern text that lists sixteen rooms; absence was established only for the chapters named in sections 1-3.
