# North Indian and pan-Indian classical Sanskrit vāstu texts on the position of the bathing room, the latrine and the household water store

Conventions used in these notes. **[RD]** = read directly (I opened the passage in the named e-text, scan-OCR or translation). **[RB: X]** = reported by the named secondary source, not opened by me. Sanskrit is given in IAST; where it was transliterated from Devanagari scan-OCR on archive.org the wording was checked by eye but small OCR slips are possible. English renderings are the named translator's when a translator is named; otherwise they are my own working translations and are marked "my tr." Directions: E, SE, S, SW, W, NW, N, NE. Edition numbering matters: the Bṛhat Saṃhitā house chapter is ch. 52 in the Tripathi/Sampurnanand text (GRETIL) and ch. 53 in Kern, Iyer and Sastri–Bhat; verse numbers differ by two.

## Key question 1. The "sixteen-room" allocation: which texts contain it, exact locators, Sanskrit wording, variants, dates; is it in the Bṛhat Saṃhitā at all?

### Takeaway
The sixteen-room ring is **not** in the Bṛhat Saṃhitā (6th c.), which allots only four corners and has no bath and no latrine. The full scheme with a bath in the east and a "house for voiding faeces" (purīṣa-tyāga-mandira) between south and south-west is a jyotiṣa-saṃhitā / muhūrta-school formulation: spelled out in the (Vṛddha-)Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā vāstu chapter vv. 164–170, compressed into one stanza in Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi 12.21 (1600 CE), given incompletely in Viśvakarmaprakāśa 2.94–98 (only 11 rooms) and in Nārada Saṃhitā 31.50–52 / Nārada Purāṇa 1.56.585–588, and then recycled by 19th–20th-century compilations. An older eight-room list (Kiraṇa-tantra as quoted by Utpala in 966 CE = Agni Purāṇa 106.18–19) puts a śrī-gṛha, not a bath, in the east and has no latrine; several texts place the bath, urinal or water differently.

### Cited Findings

**A. The fullest statement: (Vṛddha-)Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā, vāstu chapter (vāstvadhyāya), vv. 164–170**

- [RD] Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā, vāstvadhyāya vv. 164–165 (ed. Girijā Śaṅkara Śāstrī with "Mañjumatī" Hindi ṭīkā, Chaukhambha Sanskrit Bhawan 2016, pp. 411–412): *aindryāṃ diśi snānageham āgneyyāṃ pacanālayam | yāmyāyāṃ śayanaṃ veśma nairṛtyāṃ śastramandiram || vāruṇyāṃ bhojanagṛhaṃ vāyavyāṃ paśumandiram | udīcyāṃ hāṭakaṃ sadma aiśānyāṃ devamandiram ||* — my tr.: "In Indra's quarter (E) the bathing house, in the SE the cooking place, in the S the sleeping room, in the SW the weapons room; in Varuṇa's quarter (W) the dining room, in the NW the cattle house, in the N the gold (treasure) room, in the NE the gods' room." — [Vashishtha Samhita, ed. G. S. Shastri 2016 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/rbkt-vashishtha-samhita-by-vriddha-vashishtha-edited-wi)
- [RD] Same edition, vv. 167–170: *aindrāgnyor mathanaṃ madhye yāmyāgnyo rati(v.l. ghṛta)mandiram | yāmyarākṣasayor madhye purīṣatyāgamandiram || rākṣasāmbupayor madhye vidyābhyāsasya mandiram | toyeśānilayor madhye sadanaṃ rodanaṃ tataḥ || kāmopabhogasadanaṃ vāyukauberamadhyayoḥ | kaubereśānayor madhye cikitsāsadanaṃ sadā || purandareśayor madhye sarvavastususaṅgraham | sadanaṃ kārayed evaṃ kramād uktāni ṣoḍaśa ||* — my tr.: "Between E and SE the churning room; between S and SE the room of pleasure [other witnesses: ghee room]; between S and SW (Yama and the Rākṣasa quarter) the house for voiding faeces; between SW and W the study room; between W and NW the weeping room; between NW and N the room for sexual enjoyment; between N and NE the medical room; between NE and E the store of all things. Let the house be built thus: sixteen have been stated in order." (v. 166, between the two lists, is about the eight āyas.) — [Vashishtha Samhita, ed. G. S. Shastri 2016 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/rbkt-vashishtha-samhita-by-vriddha-vashishtha-edited-wi)
- [RD] Second edition, same verse numbers 164–170, same wording (latrine: *yāmyarākṣasayor madhye purīṣatyāga-mandiram*; Hindi: "दक्षिण एवं नैऋत्य कोण के मध्य में मलमूत्र त्याग स्थान"): Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā, ed. Chander Mauli Raina, vol. 2, pp. 271–272. — [Vriddha Vasishtha Samhita vol. 2, ed. Raina (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/vriddha-vasishtha-samhita-vol-2-chander-mauli-raina)
- [RD] Third edition (Lakṣmī-Veṅkaṭeśvara Press, Kalyāṇ–Bombay, p. 69–70) has the same verses with slightly garbled numbering (printed 65, 66, 67, 69, 170, 71), reading *yāmyāgnyo rati-mandiram* and *purīṣatyāga-mandiram*. — [Vasishtha Samhita, Lakshmi Venkateshwar Press (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/zvcn-vasishtha-samhita-vasishtha)

Kedar Datt Joshi (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/muhurta-chintamani-kedar-datt-joshi)

**B. The compressed stanza: Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi (Rāma Daivajña), vāstuprakaraṇa (12) v. 21, with Govinda's Pīyūṣadhārā**

- [RD] MC 12.21 (ed. Kedar Datt Joshi with Pīyūṣadhārā and "Pītāmbarā" Hindi): *snānāgnipāka-śayanāstra-bhujaś ca dhānya-bhāṇḍāra-daivata-gṛhāṇi ca pūrvataḥ syuḥ | tanmadhyatas tu mathanājya-purīṣa-vidyābhyāsākhya-rodana-ratauṣadha-sarvadhāma ||* — my tr.: "From the east onward [in the eight directions] the rooms of bathing, fire-cooking, sleeping, weapons, eating, grain, treasury and the gods; and between them, churning, ghee, faeces, study, weeping, intercourse, medicine and the room for everything." — [Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi, ed. Kedar Datt Joshi (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/muhurta-chintamani-kedar-datt-joshi)
- [RD] The same verse 21 in two other printings opens *snānasya pāka-śayanāstra-bhujeś ca …* (variant of the first compound only; allocation identical). Both Hindi glosses say "दक्षिण नैऋत्य के बीच (पुरीष) पायखाना" (between S and SW the latrine). — [Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi, Khemraj ed. (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/wzIF_muhurta-chintamani-khemraj); [Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi, Lakshmi Venkateshwar Press 1892, tr. Mahīdhara Śarmā (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/kxwd-muhurta-chintamani-piyushadhara-commentary-by-rama)
- [RD] Pīyūṣadhārā on 12.21 introduces the verse as the directional placing of the *upakaraṇa-gṛhāṇi* (utility rooms) "to be made within the house [compound] once the well has been made" (*atha kūpe kṛte gṛhamadhye kariṣyamāṇānām upakaraṇagṛhāṇāṃ dikparatvena karaṇam … āha*), glosses *purīṣa* as *viṣṭhā-tyāgasya gṛham* between S and SW, and cites as authorities Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Nārada and Viśvakarmā. — [Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi with Pīyūṣadhārā, ed. Kedar Datt Joshi (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/muhurta-chintamani-kedar-datt-joshi)
- [RD] Pīyūṣadhārā records two dissenting authorities in the same breath: "Nārada, however, states a difference: *bhāṇḍāgāraṃ tūttare syād vāyavyāṃ paśumandiram*" (treasury N, cattle house NW) and "Viśvakarmā, again, says: *uttarasyāṃ jalasthānaṃ pūrvasyāṃ śrīgṛhaṃ tathā*" — my tr.: "the water place in the north, and the śrī-gṛha (room of Śrī / wealth) in the east." — [Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi with Pīyūṣadhārā, ed. Kedar Datt Joshi (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/muhurta-chintamani-kedar-datt-joshi)
- [RD] The Vāsturatnāvalī prints the same Viśvakarmā half-verse as *uttarasyāṃ jalasnānaṃ pūrvasyāṃ śrīgṛhaṃ tathā* (its v. 129) and glosses "in the north water and the bathing room, and in the east the śrī-gṛha"; so the reading wavers between *jala-sthānam* (water place) and *jala-snānam* (water/bathing). The editor's footnote on the same page says the Nārada half-verse is "not found in the published text of the Nārada Saṃhitā". — [Vāsturatnāvalī, ed. Jugnu (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/vasturatnavali)

**C. Partial versions: Viśvakarmaprakāśa and Nārada**

- [RD] Viśvakarmaprakāśa ch. 2 vv. 94–98 (ed. Abhaya Kātyāyana, Chaukhamba Surbharati 2017, pp. 60–61): *īśānyāṃ devatāgehaṃ pūrvasyāṃ snānamandiram | āgneyyāṃ pākasadanaṃ bhāṇḍārāgāram uttare || āgneyapūrvayor madhye dadhimanthanamandiram | agnipreteśayor madhye ājyagehaṃ praśasyate || yāmyanairṛtyayor madhye purīṣatyāgamandiram | nairṛtyāmbupayor madhye vidyābhyāsasya mandiram || paścimānilayor madhye rodanārthaṃ gṛhaṃ smṛtam | vāyavyottarayor madhye ratigehaṃ praśasyate || uttareśānayor madhye auṣadhārthaṃ tu kārayet | nairṛtyāṃ sūtikāgehaṃ nṛpāṇāṃ bhūtim icchatām ||* — my tr.: "NE the gods' room, E the bathing room, SE the cooking room, N the store/treasury; between SE and E the curd-churning room; between SE and S the ghee room; between S and SW the house for voiding faeces; between SW and W the study room; between W and NW the room for weeping; between NW and N the room for intercourse; between N and NE one for medicines; in the SW the lying-in room, for kings who desire prosperity." — [Viśvakarmaprakāśa, Chaukhamba Surbharati 2017 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/vishwakarma-prakash)
- [RD] The printed Viśvakarmaprakāśa therefore names only **11** utility rooms (4 cardinal/corner + 7 intermediate) plus the SW lying-in room: it has no S bedroom, SW armoury, W dining room, NW granary or NE–E general store. The 2017 editor says so outright: "Here some verses are lost in the original; the omitted matter has therefore been taken from another text and given in brackets in the translation" (*yahāṃ mūl meṃ kuch ślok lupt haiṃ …*), and then fills the list up to sixteen. — [Viśvakarmaprakāśa, Chaukhamba Surbharati 2017 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/vishwakarma-prakash)
- [RD] Same verses, same numbers 2.94–98, in the Khemraj printing with Hindi ("दक्षिण और नैऋत के मध्य में मल के त्यागने का स्थान"), and as vv. 222–226 of the continuous numbering in the 1895 Veṅkaṭeśvara Steam Press edition with Mihiracandra's bhāṣāṭīkā. Three printings agree on bath E and latrine S–SW. — [Viśvakarmaprakāśa, Khemraj (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/EUhL_vishwakarma-prakash-khemraj); [Viśvakarmaprakāśa with Mihiracandra's ṭīkā, 1895 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/fhQD_atha-vishwakarma-prakasha-bhasha-tika-prarambhyate-by-pt.-mihir-chandra-subject-)
- [RD] The Viśvakarmā half-verse quoted by the Pīyūṣadhārā (water place N, śrī-gṛha E) is not in the printed Viśvakarmaprakāśa, which instead has *pūrvasyāṃ snānamandiram* (OCR search of the 2017 edition for *śrīgṛha* and *jalasthāna* found no Sanskrit hit). — [Viśvakarmaprakāśa, Chaukhamba Surbharati 2017 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/vishwakarma-prakash)
- [RD] Nārada Saṃhitā ch. 31 vv. 50–52 (Khemraj/Veṅkaṭeśvara 1906 with Vasatirāma Śarmā's bhāṣāṭīkā, printed p. 201): *snānāgāraṃ diśi prācyām āgneyyāṃ pacanālayam | yāmyāyāṃ śayanāgāraṃ nairṛtyāṃ śastramandiram || evaṃ kuryād idaṃ sthānaṃ kṣīrapānājyaśālikāḥ | śayyā-sūtrāstra(read mūtrāstra)-tadvidyā-bhojanā-maṅgalāśrayāḥ || dhānya-strībhoga-vittaṃ ca śṛṅgārāyatanāni ca | īśānyādikramas teṣāṃ gṛhanirmāṇakaṃ śubham ||*. Only four directions are explicit (E bath, SE kitchen, S bedroom, SW weapons); then comes an unlocated list — milk, drinking-water and ghee rooms; bed, **urine (mūtra)**, weapons and their science, dining, auspicious room; grain, women's enjoyment, wealth, adornment — "whose order begins from the NE". The 1906 Hindi gloss distributes the list by corners and puts "bed, urine, weapons, dining" in the SE (agnikoṇa). — [Nārada Saṃhitā 1906, p. 201 (sa.wikisource page scan transcription)](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/पृष्ठम्:नारदसंहिता.pdf/२०८)
- [RD] Nārada Saṃhitā, Chaukhamba ed. (Kashi Sanskrit Series 40, Rāmajanma Miśra's "Vimalā" ṭīkā), 31.50–52: same three verses, reading *śayyā-mūtrāstra-tadvidyā-bhojanā-maṅgalāśrayāḥ*; the gloss again assigns "bed, urine, weapons and dining" to the SE and footnotes the Bṛhadvāstumālā verses for comparison. — [Narad Samhita, ed. Ram Janma Mishra (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/RKpL_narad-samhita-of-narada-with-vimal-hindi-tika-by-acharya-ram-janma-mishra-kashi-)
- [RD] Nārada Saṃhitā, ed. Abhaya Kātyāyana (Chaukhamba Surbharati 2022), numbered vv. 62–65, prints two extra half-verses: *pratīcyāṃ bhojanagṛhaṃ vāyavyāṃ dhānyamandiram | kauberyāṃ devatāgāram īśānyāṃ kṣīramandiram* — i.e. W dining, NW grain, **N gods' room, NE milk room**. The editor's Hindi nevertheless paraphrases "N treasury, NE worship room" and reads the unlocated list as the intermediate rooms starting from the SE ("curd-churning, ghee, the place for voiding faeces and urine, then study, intercourse …"), i.e. he harmonises Nārada with the Vasiṣṭha scheme. — [Narada Samhita, ed. Abhay Katyayan 2022 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/gdkg-shri-narada-samhita-hindi-tika-abhay-katyayan-by-n)
- [RD] Nārada Purāṇa, Pūrvabhāga 56.585–588 (the jyotiṣa section, which reproduces Nārada-saṃhitā material): *pūrvādigeṣv alindeṣu gṛhabhedās tu ṣoḍaśa | snānāgāraṃ diśi prācyām āgneyyāṃ pākamandiram || yāmyāṃ ca śayanāgāraṃ nairṛtyāṃ śastramandiram | pratīcyāṃ bhojanagṛhaṃ vāyavyāṃ dhānyamandiram || kauberyāṃ devatāgāram īśānyāṃ kṣīramandiram | śayyā-mūtrāstra-tadvic ca bhojanaṃ maṅgalāśrayam || dhānya-strībhoga-vittaṃ ca śṛṅgārāyatanāni ca | īśānyādikramas teṣāṃ gṛhanirmāṇakaṃ śubham ||*. Note that "ṣoḍaśa" in 585ab refers to the sixteen house *types* produced by verandah (alinda) permutations (dhruva, dhānya, jaya …, listed at 581–582), not to sixteen rooms. — [Nāradapurāṇa (GRETIL, Sansknet input)](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_nAradapurANa.txt); same reading at [Nāradapurāṇa 1.56 (sa.wikisource)](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/नारदपुराणम्-_पूर्वार्धः/अध्यायः_५६)
- [RD] A further "Nārada" reading, quoted in 1571 by Nārāyaṇa in his own commentary on Muhūrta Mārtaṇḍa 6.18: *snānāgāraṃ diśi prācyām āgneyyām agnimandiram | yāmyāyāṃ śayanāgāraṃ nairṛtyāṃ vastramandiram || pratīcyāṃ bhojanāgāraṃ vāyavyāṃ paśumandiram | bhāṇḍakośaṃ tūttarasyām īśānyāṃ devamandiram* — SW = clothes (not weapons), NW = cattle, N = store/treasury, NE = gods. — [Muhūrta Mārtaṇḍa with Mārtaṇḍavallabhā, ed. Kedar Datt Joshi, p. 135 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/muhurta-martand-kedar-datt-joshi_202210)

**D. The older eight-room list has no bath and no latrine**

- [RD] Bhaṭṭotpala's commentary (completed 966 CE) on Bṛhat Saṃhitā 52.116 says Varāhamihira gave the allocation only "in general" and that "a more detailed one is read in the Tantra called Kiraṇa" (*atrācāryeṇa sāmānyena pravibhāga uktaḥ; kiraṇākhye tantre viśeṣataraḥ paṭhyate*), quoting: *pūrvasyāṃ śrīgṛhaṃ proktam āgneyyāṃ syān mahānasam | śayanaṃ dakṣiṇāyāṃ ca nairṛtyām āyudhāśrayam || bhojanaṃ paścimāyāṃ ca vāyavyāṃ dhanasañcayam | uttare dravyasaṃsthānam aiśānyāṃ devatāgṛham ||* — E śrī-gṛha, SE kitchen, S bedroom, SW armoury, W dining, NW store of wealth, N goods/treasury, NE gods' room. — [Bṛhat Saṃhitā with Bhaṭṭotpala's Vivṛti, ed. K. C. Dvivedi, pt. 2, pp. 619–620 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/lPan_brihat-samhita-of-sri-varaha-mihira-part-2-with-bhattotpala-commentary-edited-by)
- [RD] Agni Purāṇa 106.18–19 (Bibliotheca Indica text) is the same couplet with *vāyavyāṃ dhānyasaṅgrahaḥ* (granary NW): *pūrvāyāṃ śrīgṛhaṃ proktam āgneyyāṃ vai mahānasam | śayanaṃ dakṣiṇasyāṃ tu nairṛtyām āyudhāśrayam || bhojanaṃ paścimāyāṃ tu vāyavyāṃ dhānyasaṅgrahaḥ | uttare dravyasaṃsthānam aiśānyāṃ devatāgṛham ||*. — [Agnipurāṇa (GRETIL, Bibliotheca Indica ed.)](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_agnipurANa.txt)
- [RD] N. Gangadharan's translation of Agni 106.18–19: "The treasure-house should be in the east, the kitchen in the south-east, the bed-chamber in the south and the arsenal on the west [sic for south-west]. The dining (chamber) (should be) in the western part, the granary in the north-west, store-house in the north and the chamber for the deities in the north-east." — [Agni Purāṇa ch. 106, tr. Gangadharan (wisdomlib)](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-agni-purana/d/doc1083336.html)

**E. The 19th–20th-century compilations that popular books draw on**

- [RD] Vāsturatnāvalī (Jīvanātha Daivajña; ed. - The scheme is layered: 4 corners (Bṛhat Saṃhitā, 6th c.) → 8 directions without bath or latrine (Kiraṇa-tantra by 966 CE; Agni Purāṇa) → 8 directions with a bath in the east plus 8 intermediate rooms including a latrine (jyotiṣa saṃhitās of Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Nārada; Viśvakarmaprakāśa; muhūrta manuals of 1571 and 1600). The latest secure date by which the bath-in-east list existed is 1571 (Nārada quoted in the Mārtaṇḍavallabhā) and for the full sixteen with the S–SW latrine 1600–1603 (MC and Pīyūṣadhārā quoting Vasiṣṭha and Kaśyapa); how much earlier the saṃhitās themselves are I could not establish.
- Attribution of the sixteen-room list to "Viśvakarmaprakāśa" is only partly right (11 rooms in the printed text, as its own editor concedes); attribution to Varāhamihira or the Bṛhat Saṃhitā is wrong; the accurate attribution is Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā (vāstu chapter 164–170) and Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi 12.21.
- The texts and their commentators treat these as *upakaraṇa-gṛha* — ancillary rooms or outbuildings placed around a main house ("which house in which direction from the main house", Mārtaṇḍavallabhā; "to be made within the house-compound once the well is made", Pīyūṣadhārā) — and the 1571 commentary expressly allows them to be fitted "inside or outside the house" when space is short. They are not a partition plan for a single enclosed floor.
- Real disagreements, not editorial noise: E = bath vs śrī-gṛha; water = N ("Viśvakarmā" as quoted) vs NE only (Muhūrta Mārtaṇḍa) vs right/south side ("others" in the same commentary); urinal/latrine = S–SW vs S; NW = grain vs cattle; N/NE = treasury/gods vs gods/milk (Nārada).
- The Vāsturatnākara's Hindi gloss that Muhūrta Mārtaṇḍa puts the urinal in the NW contradicts the author's own commentary (south); this looks like a modern misreading of *vāyor apāṅ mūtrakam* and is a likely source of later confusion.

- Kāśyapa Saṃhitā 37.75–80 was not opened directly (known through Raina's cross-reference and the Pīyūṣadhārā's quotation).
- The Kiraṇa-tantra passage is known only from Utpala's quotation; GRETIL's Kiraṇatantra e-text covers chapters 1–6 only, so I could not locate the verses in the Tantra itself.
- No reliable composition date found for the Vasiṣṭha, Nārada and Kāśyapa jyotiṣa saṃhitās or for the Viśvakarmaprakāśa. A search snippet (not openable from this environment) attributed to a Heidelberg publication a terminus ante quem of 967 CE for the Viśvakarmaprakāśa; could not verify.
- The source of the "Viśvakarmā" half-verse (*uttarasyāṃ jalasthānaṃ pūrvasyāṃ śrīgṛhaṃ tathā*) is unidentified; it is not in the printed Viśvakarmaprakāśa.
- The Nārada list after v. 50 is textually corrupt and unlocated; the placing of *mūtra* in it cannot be fixed from the text (two Hindi glosses say SE, one modern editor says S–SW).
- I did not examine Vāstu-saukhya (Ṭoḍarānanda), Vāstupradīpa, Jyotirnibandha or Vāstusāra, which popular books also cite.

## Key question 2. What does the Bṛhat Saṃhitā Vāstuvidyā chapter itself say about rooms for bathing, latrine or water storage?

### Takeaway
One verse allots four corners (NE shrine, SE kitchen, SW household goods, NW wealth and grain) and the next gives omens for "water" in each of the eight directions (good only in N and NE). The chapter has no bathing room, no latrine, no named water room and no mirror rule; its 10th-century commentator Utpala himself says the allocation is only general.

### Inferences
- Any statement that "Varāhamihira puts the bathroom in the east" or "the toilet between south and south-west" is unsupported by the text in three independent witnesses (GRETIL/Tripathi–Kern Sanskrit, Iyer, Sastri–Bhat) and by Utpala's commentary.
- The only Bṛhat Saṃhitā material bearing on water is 52.117/53.119. Varāhamihira's word is *salila* (water) "situated in" a direction of the site; it reads as an omen about where water stands (well, tank, cistern), and Utpala and both translators take it that way. It is later authors who apply it to the household water store (Nārāyaṇa in 1571 cites this very verse, under Lalla's name, to restrict the water place to the NE).
- Utpala's "in a catuḥśāla house" shows the four-corner rule was understood for a courtyard house with four wings, where a corner is a junction between wings.

- M. Ramakrishna Bhat's Motilal translation was not usable (the archive.org OCR layer is corrupt); I used the earlier Sastri–Bhat 1946 translation instead. Kern's own translation was not consulted.
- I did not check other chapters of Utpala's commentary for further quotations on house rooms.

## Key question 3. What do the Purāṇic vāstu chapters (Matsya, Agni, Garuḍa) say about the bath, the latrine and the water room by direction?

### Takeaway
None of the three Purāṇic vāstu sections places a latrine. The Matsya Purāṇa (256.33–35) expands Varāhamihira's four corners, puts "water" beside the SE kitchen on its north side, and says the bathing pavilion is to be built outside. The Agni Purāṇa has two eight-room lists, one with a treasure room in the east (106.18–19) and one, textually corrupt, with a "washing" place in the first (east) slot (105.28–30). The Garuḍa Purāṇa passage is the layout of a Viṣṇu hermitage, with a water room in the west.

### Inferences
- The Matsya rule is the earliest explicit statement in this corpus about a bathing place in a dwelling, and it says "outside" (bahiḥ), with no compass direction. It is a direct counter-example to the claim that "the Purāṇas put the bathroom in the east".
- Matsya's *tatpārśve cottare jalam* places the household water next to the kitchen on its north side, i.e. on the east side of the house between SE and E/NE. That is a kitchen-adjacent water place, not a NE-corner rule.
- In Agni 105.28 the Sanskrit order ("in sequence from Indra's quarter … the washing-and-garland room; in Agni's quarter its kitchen …") assigns the washing place to the east and the kitchen to the SE; Gangadharan's "washing and fragrance chambers as well as the kitchen on the south-east" runs the first two slots together and leaves his eight-fold list without an east entry. This is my reading of a corrupt verse with three manuscript variants and should be treated as tentative. *Prakṣālana* is "washing", not specifically a bathroom.
- The Garuḍa passage and its Aparājitapṛcchā parallel concern a temple-hermitage or monastery compound; using them for a private dwelling is an extension the texts do not make.

## Key question 4. What do the Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra, the Aparājitapṛcchā and the Rājavallabha (with Maṇḍana's Vāstumaṇḍana) say about snāna-gṛha, dhārā-gṛha, water rooms and any latrine?

### Takeaway
All three western/central Indian treatises deal with these rooms only in their royal-palace chapters and by site-grid square, and they converge on the south-west: Bhoja puts the refuse/privy place (avaskara) on the SW-corner square and the baths and shower-houses in a pleasure garden behind the SW part of the west side; Maṇḍana's Rājavallabha puts the "faeces house" (varco-gṛha) in the SW corner, and his Vāstumaṇḍana puts bathing in the SW with the latrine "outside". Their ordinary-house chapters give no bath or latrine direction, and the shower-house (dhārā-gṛha) is expressly reserved for kings.

- [RD] Vāstumaṇḍana 5.1–2 defines "east" for this allocation as the door side, not sunrise: *yasyāṃ diśi puro dvāraṃ tāṃ prācīṃ parikalpayet | nyāsa īśāditaḥ kāryo bhojane śayane 'nyathā ||* ("whichever direction the front door is in, treat that as east; the allocation is to be made from the NE onward [on that footing]; for eating and sleeping it is otherwise"), supported by a quotation from the Vivekavilāsa: *pūrvādidigvinirdeśo gṛhe dvāravyapekṣayā | bhāskarodayadik pūrvā na vijñeyā …* ("in a house the naming of east etc. is relative to the door; the direction of sunrise is not to be taken as east …"). — [Vāstumaṇḍana, ed. A. Bhowmik (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/SlaE_vastu-mandan-of-sutradhar-mandana-by-dr.-anasuya-bhowmick-motilal-banarasidas-publishers-delhi)
- [RD] Vāstumaṇḍana 5.21, in the royal-palace allocation by grid square (introduced "Aparājite"): *… mṛge dehacintāpadaṃ bahiḥ | pitrye snānālayaṃ dyūtaṃ dvāsthe salilakelayaḥ ||* — "on Mṛga the latrine, outside; on Pitṛ (SW corner) the bath house and gaming; on Dvāstha (first west square) water sports." — [Vāstumaṇḍana, ed. A. Bhowmik (archive.org)](https://archive.org/details/SlaE_vastu-mandan-of-sutradhar-mandana-by-dr.-anasuya-bhowmick-motilal-banarasidas-publishers-delhi)
- Dates and standing as reported: Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra is an 11th-century treatise attributed to the Paramāra king Bhoja of Dhār; the Aparājitapṛcchā of Bhuvanadeva is usually placed c. 1000–1200 CE and reflects north and western Indian tradition; the Rājavallabha is attributed to Sūtradhāra Maṇḍana, a 15th-century architect said to have worked for Rāṇā Kumbha of Mewar (a Cambridge University Library manuscript record, seen only as a search-result summary; I could not open the page, so the shelfmark behind the link is unverified). — [Samarangana Sutradhara (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarangana_Sutradhara); [Aparajitaprccha (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparajitaprccha); [Cambridge Digital Library manuscript record returned by search](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-OR-00730)

### Inferences
- The treatises that are actually architectural (as opposed to astrological) agree on a zone, the south-west, for the unclean and wet functions of a palace — avaskara on the SW corner square (Bhoja), varco-gṛha in the SW corner (Rājavallabha), bath on the SW corner square and latrine on the adjoining south square "outside" (Vāstumaṇḍana), baths and shower-houses in a garden behind the SW end of the west side (Bhoja). None of them has a bath in the east.
- This is close to, but not the same as, the muhūrta-school slot "between S and SW" for the latrine, and it conflicts with the muhūrta-school bath in the east. The two traditions should not be merged into one "classical rule".
- Maṇḍana's water house is in the south (Vāstumaṇḍana 5.4) or on the right (Rājavallabha 5.40; Vāstumaṇḍana 5.8), and the 1571 Deccan commentary also records "others" who keep water, mortar, mill and hearth on the right/south side. That is a west-Indian working tradition at odds with the NE/N water rule derived from Bṛhat Saṃhitā 53.119.
- Because Maṇḍana explicitly makes "east" the door side, at least in his tradition the room list is a relational layout (front, back, left, right of the entrance), not an absolute compass prescription.
- Shower-houses (dhārā-gṛha) are garden pleasure structures restricted to kings; they are not evidence for a domestic bathroom rule.

### Gaps
- The Samarāṅgaṇa verses were read in one e-text (sa.wikisource, source edition not stated on the page); wisdomlib's separate Sanskrit e-text is behind a browser challenge and Sudarshan Kumar Sharma's 2007 English translation is not online, so 15.27 is corroborated only by a search snippet and Shukla's summary. Felix Otter's study of the residential chapters was not accessible.
- Aparājitapṛcchā sūtra 82 was read only in the Jugnū edition (the GOS scan's OCR is too garbled there); the Vāstumaṇḍana was read in one edition and its printed English translation is illegible in the OCR, so the renderings above are mine. *snāna-sūtānām* in Vāstumaṇḍana 5.4 is an awkward compound and my "bathing and childbed" is a best reading. *deha-cintā* (literally "care of the body") is taken here in its usual western-Indian sense of answering the call of nature, hence "latrine"; the legible fragments of the printed translation ("place of the … of the body") are consistent with this but could not be read with certainty.
- Whether Rājavallabha's "left/right" (vāma/dakṣiṇa) means north/south or is relative to the entrance is not settled by the verse; the Hindi translator assumes north.
- I found no bath or latrine rule for non-royal houses in the Samarāṅgaṇa or Aparājitapṛcchā; absence is from keyword search of OCR/e-text and could miss unusual vocabulary.

## Key question 5. Do any of these texts say the latrine should be outside or away from the dwelling, and at what distance or in what direction?

### Takeaway
Yes for "outside", no for distance. Maṇḍana's Vāstumaṇḍana twice says the latrine is "outside" (bahiḥ) on the south-west side; the Matsya Purāṇa puts the bathing pavilion (not a latrine) outside; the muhūrta texts put the latrine in the ring of outbuildings between S and SW or in the south and allow it "inside or outside". No vāstu text in this set gives a distance; the well-known "far from the dwelling" and "an arrow-shot to the south-west" rules come from dharma literature (Manu, Viṣṇu Purāṇa), and the Purāṇas' own conduct chapters assume open-air defecation away from houses.

### Inferences
- The south-west preference for the latrine in the vāstu and muhūrta texts mirrors the dharma-text instruction to go south-west of the house; the built latrine appears to be the old outdoor practice brought inside the compound wall at the same bearing. This link is my inference; no text read here states it.
- Where a built latrine is described at all it is in palaces or as a detached utility room; "outside" (bahiḥ) in the Vāstumaṇḍana and the "inside or outside" allowance in the Mārtaṇḍavallabhā show that an attached, in-house toilet is not what these authors had in view.
- No text in this set gives a measured distance for a latrine. Any figure quoted as "classical Vastu" for latrine distance does not come from these works.

- I did not search the Aparājitapṛcchā's town-planning sūtras or the Samarāṅgaṇa's town chapters exhaustively for communal latrines or drains; the keyword search found none.
- The Arthaśāstra and dharmaśāstra evidence was only spot-checked; a fuller treatment belongs to another researcher's scope.
- Could not verify whether Bhuvanadeva's own text (rather than Maṇḍana's adaptation headed "Aparājite") has the *dehacintāpadaṃ bahiḥ* line.

## Key question 6. Do any of these texts give rules for the arrangement of things inside a bathing room, for the direction a person faces while bathing or relieving themselves, or for mirrors?

### Takeaway
Nothing of the kind is in the vāstu passages. None of the texts examined arranges fittings inside a bathroom, prescribes a facing direction for bathing in a bath room, or gives a rule for placing mirrors in a house. The only facing rule is the dharma rule — face north by day and south by night when relieving oneself — which the Agni, Garuḍa and Nārada Purāṇas give in their conduct chapters, not in their vāstu chapters. Mirrors appear only as a named luxury building in Bhoja's glossary, in omen lists and in a temple consecration rite.

### Inferences
- Rules about where the tap, drain, seat or mirror go inside a bathroom, and about which way the user faces at a fixture, have no basis in any passage I could find in these texts; they are modern extrapolations. The one authentic facing rule is the dharma rule for open-air relief (north by day, south by night), which says nothing about room layout and predates built domestic toilets in these sources.
- If a classical warrant is wanted for toilet-seat orientation, the honest citation is Manu 4.50 and its Purāṇic echoes, labelled as a conduct rule, not as vāstu.
- Bhoja's "mirror house" shows that mirrored walls were known as a palace luxury, with no stated auspicious or inauspicious direction.

### Gaps
- Negative findings rest on keyword searches of e-texts and scan-OCR (Devanagari OCR is imperfect); unusual wording could have been missed. The texts searched for mirrors are those named in the bullet above; the Aparājitapṛcchā hits were iconographic or philosophical.
- The Aparājitapṛcchā's and Samarāṅgaṇa's chapters on furniture (śayanāsana) and house decoration were not read line by line.
- P. K. Acharya's Encyclopaedia of Hindu Architecture entries were not consulted.
